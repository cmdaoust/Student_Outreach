Electrocardiogram

What is an ECG?

Every heartbeat starts with an electrical signal. As it spreads through the heart muscle, it creates tiny voltage changes that reach your skin, where electrodes pick them up. An electrocardiogram (ECG) is a graph of that voltage over time. Each beat makes the same pattern of waves, named P, Q, R, S and T.

Our setup uses three electrodes. Two of them measure the voltage difference between their positions on your body, and the third acts as a reference. Together they give one view of the heart’s electrical activity, like the trace below.
