What is skin conductance?

Your palms and fingertips are packed with sweat glands, hundreds in every square centimetre. They are not only there to cool you down. They are wired to your sympathetic nervous system, the part of your nervous system behind the “fight or flight” reaction.

When something grabs your attention, surprises you or stresses you, your brain sends a quick signal to these glands. They push a tiny amount of sweat up into their ducts, usually far too little for you to notice.

Sweat is salty water, and salty water conducts electricity. The sensors on your fingers pass a very small current through your skin, far too small to feel, and measure how easily it flows. That is your skin conductance, measured in microsiemens (µS).

For the presenter

Sequence

Settle 15 s → Breath instructions 18 s → Deep breath → Recover 20 s → Relax (unannounced noise at 6–14 s) → Recover 20 s → Settle 8 s → Maths test announced, 15 s countdown (anticipation) → “Just kidding!” reveal, no maths → Recover 25 s. A tap-through explanation page follows the breath, the noise and the maths, so the clock keeps running while people read.

What to look for

A phasic rise starting 1–3 s after each marker, peaking 2–5 s later and recovering over 10–20 s. The deep breath and the noise give the sharpest, most reliable peaks. The maths announcement gives a peak at the marker, often followed by a slower climb during the countdown as anticipation builds. Marker times are listed at the end of the run.

Getting good signals

Let electrodes sit for a few minutes before starting. Cold hands give flat traces, so warm them first. A small share of people barely respond at all. If someone is flat, try the deep breath again on its own.

The noise

Half a second of white noise at full phone volume, with a white screen flash. It is startling but not harmful at phone-speaker level. The participant page never mentions it, so skip it for anyone who would rather not be startled.

Run one part

Name-call: the screen tells the participant to wait, then at a random moment shows a large prompt for you to clap or say their name loudly.
